PHYTAGEL HEAT GUI  --  v2.1
2.45 GHz monopole on a Phytagel phantom, with the antenna IN THE BATH.

You need MATLAB (R2022b or newer) already installed. Nothing else --
no toolboxes, no downloads, no commands to type.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS DIFFERENT FROM v1
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

v1 solved the antenna radiating into AIR above the gel. Photographs of the
50 W bench run show it FULLY IMMERSED. Because the bath has almost the
same permittivity as the gel, the interface nearly disappears
electrically and most of the power goes UP INTO THE BATH instead of down
into the gel.

                            into the gel     into the bath
    v1  air above               99.6 %            0 %
    v2  tap/deionised bath      46.4 %           51.3 %
    v2  0.9 % saline bath       39.2 %           60.7 %

So v1 over-predicted the heating badly. On the photographed run
(50 W, 6 min) v1 said the peak hit 475 C; v2 says 171-185 C.

BE HONEST ABOUT WHAT THIS FIXES. v2 removes about 2.8x of the error and
no more. 171 C still boils, and the photographed gel plainly did not.
The rest must come from things v2 does not model -- above all how much
power actually leaves the generator and reaches the antenna, which was
assumed (75 %) and never measured. See "What changed in v2.md".


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT v2.1 ADDED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE GREY WEDGE IS GONE.

   Both maps used to have a grey semicircle punched out of them at the
   feed, because the model's antenna current stopped dead there and the
   deposition went infinite. The photographs show nothing like it: the
   gel there is heated smoothly, the hottest part sits a couple of
   millimetres along and a couple down, and the heated blob reaches BACK
   past the junction under the braid.

   The current does not stop at the feed. It RETURNS along the outside of
   the bare braid -- there is no ground plane and no balun on that bench,
   so the thing is a dipole whose second arm is the coax. Model that and
   there is no point charge, nothing has to be blanked, and the peak the
   window reports is a real prediction instead of the value on the edge
   of an arbitrary sphere. It also stops depending on the grid: 1.2 %
   across three grid sizes, against 32 % before.

   New control:  Feed (the coax braid) -> braid arm (mm), 12 mm default.
   Set it to 0 for the old truncated current, grey wedge and all; the
   "v1/v2 feed" preset does exactly that.

   On the photographed run the peak goes from 185 C to 151 C. It still
   boils. Point 1 above is still the thing to go and measure.

2. PULSING.

   New controls:  drive (continuous / pulsed), pulse period, duty cycle.

   The power box is then the power DURING a pulse; the mean is duty times
   that, and the line above the maps gives both. Compare pulsed against
   continuous at equal MEAN power, never at equal generator power.

   Watch the second number in that line. A slow pulse train can finish
   deep in an off-period, so the temperature at the end of the run is not
   the worst the gel saw -- 50 W at 50 % duty on a 30 s period reads
   72 C at the end and 102 C at its worst.

   Slow pulsing at a fixed mean power is strictly worse than continuous.
   That is not a quirk of this window; it is what the heat write-up
   already said, and it is now something you can watch happen.

3. THE TIME BAR IS LINEAR IN SECONDS.

   It used to be an index into a log-spaced list of times, so a quarter
   of its travel covered the first second of a sixty second run and the
   rest covered the other fifty-nine. The frames are now on a round fixed
   increment -- 2 s for a 60 s run, 10 s for six minutes -- and the bar
   is labelled in seconds. Half way along is half way through.

PICK ONE OF THESE TWO. Either works.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

  (1) INSTALL IT PROPERLY  -- recommended

      Double-click        Phytagel Heat GUI v2.mltbx

      MATLAB opens and asks to install. Click Install.
      The GUI then lives in MATLAB's APPS tab: click APPS, then the
      button labelled  heat_gui .

      NOTE. This installs as version 2.0.0 of the SAME add-on as v1, so
      it REPLACES v1 rather than sitting beside it. That is deliberate:
      the two share every function name, and having both on the path at
      once would silently run whichever came first. You lose nothing --
      set "above the gel" to "air" in the window and you have v1 back,
      exactly.

  (2) JUST OPEN IT, DON'T INSTALL ANYTHING

      Windows:  double-click   Open the GUI (Windows).bat
      Mac:      double-click   Open the GUI (Mac).command

      Installs nothing and touches nothing. This is also the way to run
      v1 and v2 side by side if you really want both at once.


Either way the window takes about 30 seconds to appear the first time.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE NEW CONTROLS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

A "Bath (what the antenna sits in)" section (v2):

  above the gel      water (v2)  or  air (v1 behaviour)
  bath salt (% w/v)  0 = deionised or tap, 0.9 = physiological saline

The bath salinity of the real experiment was never measured, so try
both. It matters less than you would expect: pure water is ALREADY
lossy at 2.45 GHz (that is how a microwave oven works), so the two ends
give 46 % and 39 % into the gel -- close enough that not knowing does
not change the conclusion.

Under the maps there is now a "bath" line saying how much of the
delivered power went up into the bath instead of into the gel.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECKING IT YOURSELF
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

In MATLAB, from the code folder:

    verify_v2             the checks, about 2 minutes
    run_v2_comparison     the version-by-version table, about 100 s

The checks are worth knowing about, because two of them failed when
first written and that is how the bugs were caught:

  1  tangential E continuous across the gel surface     1.6e-17
  2  "air" reproduces v1 to machine precision           exact
  3  the result does not depend on how deep a bath
     column is integrated                               drift 5e-8
  4  the power budget closes                            exact
  5  in a uniform medium with the wire on the surface,
     equal power above and below                        1.000000
  6  the size of the point charge at the feed, read
     straight off the spectral tail                     1.0014 -> 0.0136
     ... and the peak temperature, over three grids     32 % -> 1.2 %
  7  pulsing delivers exactly the energy it claims,
     for periods from 0.2 s to 30 s                     1.9e-14


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STILL TRUE FROM v1
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Compare a thermocouple against the PROBE POINT. The peak is now a real
prediction rather than an artefact, but it is still 3 mm below the
surface next to the feed junction, which is not somewhere you can put a
thermocouple.

Do not put a probe within 2 mm of the feed. Put it under the middle of
the element, around x = 6 mm.

"How to use the GUI.md" documents every control -- its temperatures are
v1 numbers and read high; the preset table in it has been updated to
v2.1, the rest has not.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF YOU CHANGE THE MODEL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Run  build_mltbx  (it sits beside this file) to repackage code/ into the
.mltbx. The .mltbx is a COPY, so without a rebuild anyone installing it
gets the old model while anyone using the .bat gets the new one, and
nothing in the window would say so.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
TO UNINSTALL (route 1 only)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

In MATLAB: HOME tab -> Add-Ons -> Manage Add-Ons -> Phytagel Heat GUI
-> the three dots -> Uninstall.
